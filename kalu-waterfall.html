D1

Day 1: Bangalore to Belagavi

Bangalore → Belagavi | ~500 km | NH48 Mumbai Highway

I left Bangalore around 10:30 AM on my KTM Adventure 390, hitting the Bangalore-Mumbai Highway (NH48). The initial stretch through Karnataka was smooth — four-lane highway, moderate traffic, and the excitement of the open road keeping me pumped. The Adventure 390 was eating miles effortlessly.

But by the time I crossed Hubli, the sky turned dark and the monsoon showed no mercy. What started as a light drizzle turned into absolute sheets of rain. Visibility dropped, my riding gear was getting soaked through, and the highway had ankle-deep water in stretches. I had planned to reach Pune that day, but it was impossible.

I made the call to halt at Belagavi. Found a decent hotel near the highway, dried my gear as much as I could, and crashed for the night. The rain didn't stop — I could hear it hammering the window all night. I knew Day 2 was going to be even crazier.

Pro Tip: If you're riding from Bangalore, start by 6 AM to maximize daylight. The NH48 is excellent but rain can slow you down significantly. Belagavi has plenty of hotels near the highway — book in advance during monsoon season.

D2

Day 2: Belagavi to Pune — 9 Hours of Rain

Belagavi → Pune | ~400 km | Non-stop monsoon riding

I woke up at 5:30 AM and it was still raining. Not the pleasant Instagram-kind of rain — I'm talking about 9 hours of continuous, bone-soaking, visibility-destroying monsoon rain. I geared up, waterproofed my luggage as best I could, and hit the highway.

The Bangalore-Mumbai Highway between Belagavi and Pune is scenic but in this weather, it was a different beast. Truck spray made visibility near zero, my gloves were soaked within the first hour, and water was seeping into my boots. The KTM Adventure 390 handled brilliantly though — the upright riding position and wind protection made a huge difference.

I barely stopped. Quick tea breaks at highway dhabas where I'd squeeze water out of my gloves, then back on the road. Pune felt like an oasis when I finally reached — warm food, dry clothes, and the realization that the hardest highway stretch was behind me.

The Junnar Detour — Leaving the Highway Behind

Pune → Nashik Highway exit → Junnar | ~60 km off-road vibes

After crossing Pune, I hit the Pune-Nashik Highway — and this is where the real journey began. I had to get off the national highway and take a 60 km detour towards Junnar, a small village that's the gateway to Kalu Waterfall.

This route was a completely different world. Nature, mountains, barely any shops, and the rain was still pouring. The highway chaos was behind me, replaced by winding village roads cutting through lush green valleys. The further I rode, the fewer people I saw. It was just me, my KTM, and the sound of rain on my helmet.

Junnar itself is a quiet town nestled in the mountains. If you need fuel, water, or a quick snack — stock up here because beyond this point, civilization thins out dramatically. The locals told me the road ahead gets even more scenic — and they weren't kidding.

Malshej Ghat — Next Level Beauty

Junnar → Ghat Section → Madha Village → Malshej Ghat

Past Junnar, I entered a medium ghat section where clouds and mist surrounded the mountains completely. I'm talking about that surreal fog where you can barely see 20 feet ahead, and mountains just appear and disappear into the clouds. It was eerie and magical at the same time.

I reached Madha village and found a decent homestay for ₹2000 — a simple room with a warm bed and hot food, which after two days of rain riding, felt like a 5-star resort. I offloaded all my luggage and took an hour's rest.

But I couldn't sit still. Malshej Ghat was just 5 km from Madha village, so I hopped back on the KTM (without luggage this time — the bike felt so nimble!) and went to explore. And let me tell you something — I've been to Munnar, Goa, Kanyakumari, and Rameshwaram, but Malshej Ghat during monsoon is NEXT LEVEL.

Unlimited waterfalls surrounded by mountains — not one or two, but literally hundreds of waterfalls of all sizes cascading down every mountain face. The roads were scary — narrow ghat roads with sheer drops, fog reducing visibility, and water flowing across the road in multiple places. But the beauty was absolutely overwhelming.

The Final Stretch — Reaching Kalu Waterfall

Malshej Ghat Arch → Police Checkpoint → Dense Forest → Kalu Waterfall

After multiple pitstops and taking beautiful waterfall photos all through Malshej Ghat, I finally entered the arch of Kalu Waterfall. This is where the real adventure begins.

🏍️ Having a Bike = Massive Advantage

After 4 km of dense forest riding, there's a police checkpoint where you pay ₹50 entry fee. From this point, ALL four-wheelers must stop. They simply can't go further. But since I had my KTM Adventure 390, I was allowed to ride another 4 km deeper into the forest. That's 4 km of walking saved while others were sweating on foot. If you're planning this trip — a bike is the way to go.

Those extra 4 km on the bike through the dense forest were surreal — thick canopy overhead, the sound of water everywhere, barely any sunlight penetrating through. And then I had to cross 2 rivers through the dense forest. Not small streams — proper rivers with knee-deep water flowing fast. The KTM handled it like a champ.

Then came the final approach. I had to pay ₹100 to cross the massive river — this is the one you see in all the Instagram videos. A local guides you across the safest path. And after crossing, one final 600-meter trek through slippery rocks and wet trails...

And then — THAT MOMENT. I witnessed the magical Kalu Waterfall. As seen on Instagram? NO. It was MUCH MORE BEAUTIFUL in real life.

"As seen on Instagram? No. Much more beautiful."

Drone Shots — Capturing the Magic

Flying the drone at Kalu Waterfall for cinematic aerial footage

After that incredible moment of witnessing Kalu Waterfall in person, I pulled out my drone. Flying a drone in monsoon weather near a massive waterfall is risky — the moisture, the wind from the falls, the rain — but I had to capture this.

And the footage was absolutely worth it. Seeing the waterfall from above — the massive cascade dropping from the mountain top, the river flowing through the forest, the clouds drifting across the peaks — it was cinematic gold. The kind of footage that makes people go "WHERE IS THIS?!"

I spent about 20 minutes flying, carefully navigating through the mist and drizzle. Every angle was a postcard. If you're a content creator or just someone who loves capturing beauty — Kalu Waterfall is a dream location for drone photography.

Frequently Asked Questions About Kalu Waterfall

Everything people ask before visiting Kalu Waterfall and Malshej Ghat — answered from real experience.

From Bangalore, take the Mumbai Highway (NH48) via Belagavi and Pune. After Pune, take the Pune-Nashik Highway and exit towards Junnar (60 km off the highway). From Junnar, ride through the Malshej Ghat route to Madha village. The total distance is approximately 1000 km one way. By bike, it takes 2 days with a recommended overnight halt at Belagavi. By car, it's a 16-18 hour drive. The nearest railway station is Pune, and the nearest airport is Pune Airport (PNQ).

The entry fee for Kalu Waterfall is ₹50 per person at the police checkpoint inside the dense forest area. Additionally, you need to pay ₹100 to cross the massive river near the waterfall (locals help you cross safely). So the total cost per person is ₹150. If you're coming by car, parking is ₹50. Two-wheelers can go 4 km beyond the checkpoint, which is a huge advantage since car visitors have to walk that entire distance.

Yes! This is one of the biggest advantages of visiting Kalu Waterfall on a bike. After the police checkpoint (where you pay ₹50 entry), all four-wheelers must stop. But two-wheelers — especially adventure bikes like KTM Adventure 390, Royal Enfield Himalayan, etc. — are allowed to ride another 4 km deeper into the dense forest. This saves you a long walk and gets you much closer to the waterfall. After those 4 km, you still need to cross 2 rivers and do a 600-meter trek, but having the bike cuts significant walking time.

The best time to visit Kalu Waterfall is during the monsoon season from July to September. August sees the waterfall at its maximum volume and the entire Malshej Ghat region transforms with hundreds of waterfalls. However, be prepared for heavy rainfall, slippery ghat roads, and dense fog. The waterfall significantly reduces in volume after September and may completely dry up by summer (March-May). Weekends in August are extremely crowded, so plan for a weekday visit if possible.

Kalu Waterfall is approximately 8-10 km from the Malshej Ghat main area. After entering through the Malshej Ghat arch, you ride 4 km through dense forest to reach a police checkpoint. From there, bikes can continue another 4 km, after which you cross 2 rivers and do a final 600-meter trek to reach the waterfall. Madha village, which is the closest place to stay, is about 5 km from the Malshej Ghat viewpoint area.

The Kalu Waterfall trek is rated moderate. The actual trek distance is only 600 meters, but it involves crossing 3 rivers (2 on the way, 1 major crossing near the waterfall), walking through dense forest trails, and navigating slippery rocks during monsoon. The river crossings can be challenging during heavy rain when water levels rise. If you have a bike, you can reduce significant walking distance. Wear sturdy trekking shoes with good grip, carry waterproof bags, and avoid visiting during extremely heavy rainfall warnings.

Madha village near Malshej Ghat is the closest and most convenient place to stay. I stayed at a decent homestay for ₹2000 per night — basic but clean with hot food. There are a few more homestay options in Madha ranging from ₹1500-2500. If you want more hotel options, Junnar town (60 km away) has better facilities. There are also some premium resorts along Malshej Ghat if you prefer luxury. During peak monsoon weekends, accommodation fills up fast — book at least a week in advance.

It's challenging but doable with the right preparation. I rode through 9 hours of continuous rain from Belagavi to Pune. The NH48 highway is well-maintained but waterlogging and truck spray are major concerns. The ghat roads near Malshej Ghat are narrow with fog reducing visibility to 20 feet. Key safety tips: 1) Use a good rain suit and waterproof riding gear, 2) Ensure your bike has good tires with adequate tread, 3) Don't ride after dark in ghat sections, 4) Check weather forecasts for extremely heavy rainfall days, 5) Ride at a comfortable speed — there's no rush.
